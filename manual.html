BlindSpot manual

Build 2026.9.6

BlindSpot is a Spotify player for Windows and macOS, designed to be used entirely from the keyboard with a screen reader. It's portable, so there's nothing to install. Just run it from its folder.

This manual shows you what BlindSpot can do and how to do it. Where information comes from somewhere other than Spotify, it tells you where, and it explains what BlindSpot saves on your computer.

Finding your way around

At the top of the main window is a menu bar, followed by a row of tabs such as Search, Liked Songs and Playlists. Below those are the controls for the tab you're on, a list of results, and finally a Now Playing area. Press Tab and Shift+Tab to move between them.

In a list, press Enter on a song, chapter or episode to play it. Press Enter on an album, artist, playlist, audiobook or podcast to open it and see what's inside. To go back, press Backspace or Alt+Left, and you'll return to the same spot in the list you came from.

Most items can do more than play or open. To see everything you can do with one, press the Applications key or Shift+F10 to open its context menu.

Spotify sends long lists a page at a time. When there's more to come, the last item in the list is Load more or Show more. Press Enter on it and the next page is added to the bottom.

You can only run one copy of BlindSpot at a time. If you try to start a second one, it tells you BlindSpot is already running.

Keyboard shortcuts

These are the shortcuts BlindSpot starts with. You can change most of them in Keyboard Manager, which is described under Preferences. On macOS, if the menu shows Command for a command, use Command in place of Ctrl.

Getting around

  • Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9: go straight to a tab. In order, they are Search, Liked Songs, Queue, Playlists, Recently Played, Audiobooks, Podcasts, Saved Albums and Discover.
  • Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab: move to the next or previous tab.
  • Ctrl+F: go to the search box.
  • Ctrl+B: open your bookmarks.
  • Ctrl+Shift+G: search for concerts.
  • Ctrl+Comma: open Preferences.
  • Enter: play a song or episode, or open an album, playlist or other collection.
  • Backspace or Alt+Left: go back.
  • Applications key or Shift+F10: open the context menu. On macOS, press Option+M.

Playback

  • F4: play the selected song, album, playlist or artist. If you're not in a list, F4 plays the song you were listening to last time.
  • F5: go back to the start of the song. Press it twice quickly to go back to the previous song.
  • F6 and F8: go back or forward five seconds.
  • F7: pause or resume. When you've just started BlindSpot, F7 can also pick up where you left off last time. See Preferences to turn this on.
  • F9: go to the next song.
  • Space: pause or resume. On a button, checkbox or edit box, Space does its usual job instead.
  • Shift+F4 and Shift+F5: turn the volume down or up by five percent.
  • Shift+F7: mute or unmute.
  • Shift+F6 and Shift+F8: jump to the previous or next verse or chorus. This is explained under Lyrics.
  • Ctrl+R: change the repeat mode.
  • Ctrl+S: turn shuffle on or off.
  • Ctrl+J: jump to a particular time in the song.
  • Ctrl+Shift+J: set the sleep timer.
  • Ctrl+Shift+D: choose which device the music plays on.

Songs and your library

  • Ctrl+Q: add the selected songs to the queue.
  • Ctrl+L: like or unlike the selected song.
  • Ctrl+Shift+L: like or unlike the song that's playing.
  • Ctrl+Shift+A: add the selected songs to one of your playlists. They keep the order you see them in.
  • Ctrl+Shift+N: create a new playlist.
  • Ctrl+Shift+B: bookmark your place in what's playing.
  • Ctrl+Shift+O or Ctrl+Enter: open the album the selected song is from. If you're not in a list, Ctrl+Shift+O opens the album of the song that's playing.
  • Delete: remove a song from Liked Songs or from one of your playlists, or remove a playlist from your library. You'll be asked to confirm first.

Hearing what's playing

  • Ctrl+Shift+I: hear the name of the song that's playing.
  • Ctrl+Shift+U: hear what's coming up next.
  • Ctrl+Shift+E: hear how far into the song you are.
  • Ctrl+Shift+T: hear how long the song is.
  • Ctrl+Y: show the lyrics for the selected song, or for the song that's playing.
  • Ctrl+Shift+F: refresh the list you're in.

Working with lists

The quickest way to reach an item is to type the first few letters of its name. Type them quickly, one after the other, and BlindSpot jumps to the first match. Wait a moment before typing again to start a new search.

Your library and playlists also have a Filter this list box. You'll find it just before Now Playing as you tab around. As you type, the list narrows to show only matching titles, artists, albums or shows. It doesn't matter whether you use capitals or accents, so typing beyonce finds Beyoncé. The filter only searches what has already loaded, so load more first if you need to. To see the whole list again, clear the box. In a filtered playlist, F4 plays only the songs you can see, starting from the one you're on.

To change the order of a list, open the View menu and choose Sort by. You can sort by title, artist, album, length or date added, as long as Spotify provides that information. Choose Descending in the same menu to reverse the order, or Original order to put everything back. Sorting only changes what you see in BlindSpot. Your playlists on Spotify stay exactly as they were.

The Queue and Recently Played can't be sorted, because their order is the whole point. The Queue shows what will play next, and Recently Played shows what you heard, in the order you heard it.

Searching

Search can find songs, albums, artists, playlists, podcasts, podcast episodes, audiobooks and audiobook chapters. Before you search, choose which of these you're looking for. You'll see the first 20 matches straight away, and if there are more, you'll find Load more results at the end of the list.

Press Enter to open or play a result. For everything else you can do with it, open its context menu.

Related albums

Enjoying an album and want more like it? Open the context menu on the album, or on any of its songs, and choose Related albums. Press Enter to open a suggestion or F4 to play it. Backspace brings you back to where you started.

Browsing by genre

Search also has a Genre box for exploring songs, albums or artists by style. Choose one of the suggested genres or type your own. To narrow the results further, type something in the ordinary search box as well.

The genres come from Last.fm, where listeners tag music with whatever words they like. That makes them a helpful guide, but not an official label. BlindSpot tells you when results come from Last.fm, and finds each one on Spotify before you play or save it.

Not sure what to type? Start from something you already like. Open the context menu on a song or artist and choose Browse genres (Last.fm). You'll see the tags listeners use most, each with a score out of 100 showing how strongly it fits. Choose a tag to find more songs or artists like it. Tags that aren't really genres, such as the artist's name, the song title, a year, or an opinion like seen live, are left out. Songs that few people listen to may have no tags at all.

Finding covers

To hear other artists' versions of a song, open its context menu and choose Find covers (MusicBrainz). MusicBrainz is a free, open music database. Unlike Spotify, it knows which recordings are versions of the same song, and which of those are covers.

The versions appear in the Search tab. Known covers come first, sorted by artist, and each one ends with the word cover. Next come other recordings of the same song. After those are recordings with the same title that MusicBrainz hasn't yet linked to the song, which often happens with new releases. BlindSpot compares their length with your song to leave out ones that are clearly different. Novelty versions, such as 8-bit arrangements, come last. When you're done, Backspace takes you back to where you were.

Live, video and karaoke recordings aren't included. Neither are recordings by the artist you started from, unless you started from a cover. In that case, the original is included.

MusicBrainz doesn't rank versions by popularity, so a famous cover may not be near the top. It also asks programs not to send it more than one request a second, so a search can take up to 20 seconds. For very popular songs, BlindSpot looks through the first 300 recordings, and the status line tells you how many it checked. If MusicBrainz doesn't know the song at all, BlindSpot tells you.

Each version shows its artist, title and year. BlindSpot doesn't look for it on Spotify until you play it, queue it, like it or open its menu. MusicBrainz knows about many recordings that aren't on Spotify, so now and then you'll be told a version can't be found.

Playlists

Playing and selecting songs

Press Enter on a playlist to open it, or F4 to play it. Inside a playlist, F4 plays from the selected song to the end. If you follow a playlist that you can't change, BlindSpot announces it as read only.

To work with several songs at once, select them with Ctrl+Space. On macOS, use VoiceOver's selection commands. To select every song in the list, press Ctrl+A, or Command+A on macOS. You'll also find Select all in the Edit menu and the context menu. Once songs are selected, Ctrl+Q adds them all to the queue.

You can also copy songs from one playlist to another. Select them and press Ctrl+C, or Command+C on macOS. To move them out of one of your own playlists instead, press Ctrl+X. Then open the playlist you want them in and press Ctrl+V. Songs you cut stay in the original playlist until they've safely arrived in the new one.

To remove a song from one of your playlists, press Delete. In your list of playlists, Delete removes the whole playlist from your library. Either way, you'll be asked to confirm first.

Organising playlists

A playlist's context menu lets you open it, play it, create a new playlist, or remove it from your library. If the playlist is yours, you can rename it too.

In your own playlists, each song's context menu has a Move submenu. From there you can move the song up or down, to the top or bottom, or to a particular position. The song stays selected as it moves, and BlindSpot tells you where it ended up. You can't rearrange playlists that belong to someone else.

A song's context menu also has Copy to playlist. In your own playlists, it has Move to another playlist as well. These work on the songs you've selected, or on the current song if nothing is selected. As with cutting and pasting, a song you move stays put until it has reached the other playlist.

Swapping in a different version

Sometimes a playlist has the wrong recording of a song, perhaps a live version or a remaster you don't like. To find a better one, choose Find alternate versions from the song's context menu. BlindSpot finds other recordings of the song by the same artist, leaving out re-releases of the same recording. Press Enter on one to hear it. If you like it, press Tab to reach the Replace button, and it takes the old one's place in the playlist. You can also choose Replace playlist track from its context menu.

Find alternate versions is also in other song menus, and under Playback, Now playing. From there, Enter just plays the version you choose, and your playlists aren't touched.

Mixes of similar songs

A mix is an endless stream of songs like one you choose, suggested by Last.fm. From a song's context menu, you can look at a mix, start one playing, or add one to the end of your queue. Ctrl+Shift+M shows a mix for the selected song, and Ctrl+Shift+P starts one playing. For the song that's playing, you'll find the same commands in the Go menu.

Looking at a mix shows up to 50 suggestions, 20 at a time, and doesn't interrupt what you're listening to. Starting a mix plays the first song straight away, and the rest are queued as BlindSpot finds them. When only about ten songs are left, BlindSpot finds more like the song that's playing at the time. The mix carries on like this until you play something else. You won't hear the same song twice in one session, and repeat is turned off when a mix starts.

Albums

Press Ctrl+8 to see the albums saved in your library. Press Enter to open one and Backspace to come back. To save an album to your library, or remove it, press Ctrl+L.

Each song in an album shows its track number and title, and any guest artists. If the album has more than one disc, you'll hear the disc number too. Press Enter to play just that song, or F4 to play the album from that song onwards.

For the fine print, choose Album and recording information from the context menu of a song or album, or from Playback, Now playing. You'll see whether it's an album, single or compilation, and its release date, record label and copyright. For a song, you'll also see its track and disc number, its length, and whether it's explicit. When Spotify has them, the ISRC and UPC codes are shown as well.

Discover

Press Ctrl+9 for Discover. Here you can explore new releases, keep up with artists and authors you follow, and browse charts and countdowns. Use the buttons at the top to choose what you'd like to see. Only the options that suit your choice are available, and the list doesn't change until you press Search.

A lot of what Discover shows comes from outside Spotify. BlindSpot only looks for a song on Spotify when you want to play it or do something else with it. There's more about this under Charts from outside Spotify, below.

New releases

Choose New releases, then choose Songs or Albums, a genre and a country. Under Released within, choose how recent the releases should be. Then press Search. The results come from the iTunes Store charts for that genre and country, with the newest first.

To see new releases from one artist, type their name in the keyword box. This is handy for smaller artists who don't make the charts. The genre is ignored when you search this way, but Released within still counts, so choose Any time if you'd like older releases too.

Artists and authors you follow

BlindSpot can watch for new music from artists you like and new audiobooks from authors you like. To follow someone, open the context menu on one of their songs, albums or audiobooks and choose Follow for new releases. To follow whoever's playing, choose Playback, Now playing, Follow artist or author for new releases. To see who you're following, or to stop following someone, choose File, Followed artists and authors.

To see what they've released, choose Followed artists and authors in Discover. BlindSpot uses the country from your Spotify account, or Australia if your account doesn't have one. Press Enter to open a release or F4 to play it. Ctrl+L saves it to your library, or removes it if it's already there.

BlindSpot also checks for new releases by itself a few seconds after it starts, and tells you about any it hasn't mentioned before. If you'd rather it didn't, you can turn this off in Preferences.

New audiobooks come from Apple's catalogue. BlindSpot only lists books marked Unabridged or Abridged, which leaves out translated editions. If Spotify has the book, it opens in the Audiobooks tab.

Apple Music most played

This chart shows the songs or albums played most on Apple Music in the country you choose. It's based on Apple's own listening data, so it reflects what everyone on Apple Music is really playing rather than a sample. Apple publishes up to 100 entries, and you get them all at once. Each one gives its position, title, artist and year. For an album, press Enter to open it on Spotify or F4 to play it.

The chart starts out on your Spotify account's country, or on Australia if Apple doesn't have a chart for it, and you can change the country whenever you like. The status line tells you which chart and country you're looking at, and when BlindSpot loaded it. Apple only gives chart positions. It doesn't say how many times songs were played, or what period the chart covers.

Don't be surprised to find a song from decades ago halfway down the chart. People keep playing their old favourites, and those plays count just as much as plays of new releases.

Charts and countdowns

Choose Charts and countdowns, then choose one of these under Chart.

US Billboard Hot 100 (experimental). Type a date as year, month and day, such as 1985-07-13 or 19850713, and you'll see the chart for that week. The chart comes from mhollingshead/billboard-hot-100, a project on GitHub run by volunteers. It isn't official and isn't part of BlindSpot, so it could change or disappear.

Triple J Hottest 100. Choose a countdown from the list. There's one for every year, and specials such as the Hottest 100 of Australian Songs, Like A Version, and the all-time countdowns. Each entry gives its place, artist, title and the year it was released, all taken from the ABC's official archive. Remember that the Hottest 100 is a vote by listeners, not a sales chart.

ABC Classic 100. ABC Classic's yearly countdowns, voted for by listeners, from 2001 to the most recent. Each year has its own theme. Each entry gives its place, the piece and the composer. When you play one, BlindSpot finds a recording of it on Spotify. For a long work such as a symphony or opera, you may get a single movement or excerpt rather than the whole piece.

ABC Radio National Top Books. The 2025 countdown of the top 100 books of the 21st century. You can also choose its companion list, The Ones That Got Away, which covers places 101 to 200, or both lists together. Each entry gives its place, title and author. BlindSpot only looks for an audiobook when you open or play a book, so browsing the list doesn't use up your audiobook listening time. Which audiobooks you can hear depends on your Spotify plan and country. As the ABC publishes new countdowns, they can be added to this list.

Number ones. The songs and albums that reached number one in the United Kingdom, Australia and New Zealand. Choose a country, choose Songs or Albums under Show, then type a year or a date.

Type a year, such as 1985, to list every number one from that year. Each one appears once, with the number of weeks it spent at the top that year, and its total weeks if they're different. The longest-running number ones come first, or you can list them in the order they reached the top. Type a full date, such as 1985-07-13, to find out what was number one that week.

UK singles go back to 1952 and UK albums to 1956. Australian singles go back to 1940 and Australian albums to 1965. In New Zealand, both go back to 1980. These lists come from Wikipedia, which bases them on each country's official charts and shares them under the CC BY-SA licence. They only cover number ones, not the full charts. Because anyone can edit Wikipedia, they may also contain the odd mistake.

Charts from outside Spotify

If BlindSpot looked up every song in a chart as soon as the chart appeared, it would be slow, and it would soon hit Spotify's limit on requests. Instead, it looks a song up the first time you do something with it. That might be playing it, queueing it, liking it, adding it to a playlist, opening its album or menu, or starting a mix from it. The lookup takes a moment, and BlindSpot lets you know it's searching.

Until a song has been looked up, BlindSpot can't show Spotify details such as its album or length. It also can't tell you whether Spotify has the song. If Spotify doesn't, you'll find out when you try to play it. If Spotify asks BlindSpot to slow down, you'll be told how long to wait.

You can filter and sort Discover results just like any other list, and the filter never hides the Load more item.

Podcasts

BlindSpot can play podcasts from Spotify, and podcasts you've subscribed to in another app. Spotify keeps track of where you're up to in its podcasts, and BlindSpot keeps track of the rest.

Spotify podcasts

Press Ctrl+7 for Podcasts. You'll see the shows you follow and the episodes you've saved, and every episode picks up where you left off.

To find something new, choose a category such as Comedy, News, Science or Technology, then press Browse podcasts. You'll get Spotify's best matches for that category rather than a complete directory. Each show tells you who makes it. Press Enter to see its episodes, or open its context menu to read what it's about. Backspace takes you back to your own shows.

You can also look for podcasts in the Search tab. Choose Podcasts to find whole shows, or Podcast episodes to find individual episodes. For more results, choose Show more at the end of the list. When you open a show, you'll see its latest 50 episodes. To see older ones, choose Show more episodes at the end. BlindSpot adds them to the bottom of the list and takes you to the first new one.

You can narrow your podcasts down by language, or by whether you've started, finished or not yet played an episode. If Spotify doesn't know whether you've played an episode, it's marked Unknown. When there are more pages still to load, the counts only include what's loaded so far.

Podcasts from other apps

Most podcast apps can export your subscriptions as an OPML file. To bring them into BlindSpot, choose File, Import podcast subscriptions from OPML. Any podcasts you already have are skipped. These subscriptions belong to BlindSpot and aren't connected to your Spotify account.

Their episodes stream straight from the podcast's publisher, and you control them with the same keys as everything else. Some publishers don't allow skipping forward and back, so this may not always work.

Downloading episodes

To save an episode to your computer, choose Download episode from its context menu. BlindSpot downloads it from the podcast's public feed, so private episodes and Spotify-only shows can't be downloaded.

Transcripts

To read along with an episode, choose Show transcript from its context menu. If the podcast publishes its own transcript, that's what you'll see.

If it doesn't, BlindSpot can make one for you with Whisper, a speech recognition program. The first time, you'll be asked whether to download Whisper, which is about 150 MB. After that, transcription happens entirely on your computer, and the text appears bit by bit as Whisper works through the episode. Private and Spotify-only episodes can't be transcribed.

Podcasts sometimes change their ads from one download to the next, which would throw the timing out. To avoid this, BlindSpot keeps its own copy of the episode and plays that while you read.

To stop part way through, press Tab to reach Stop transcription. You'll keep the text that has been written so far.

Moving around a transcript works much like the Lyrics window. Press Space to play from the line you're on, and Enter to pause or resume. Tick Follow transcript playback and your cursor keeps pace with what's being said. The playback keys and your lyrics shortcuts work here too.

To read a transcript in another language, first set up DeepL, as described under Lyrics. Once transcription has finished, choose Translation in the list above the transcript, or press Translate with DeepL. When the translation arrives, BlindSpot switches to it. To go back, choose Original. The translation is saved, so it's there straight away next time. Playback still follows the timings of the original.

Transcribing your own recordings

Whisper can transcribe any audio or video file, not just podcasts. It's handy for interviews, meetings and lectures. Choose File, Transcribe local audio, and pick the file. It opens in the same transcript window, and Space plays from the line you're on. BlindSpot plays your original file and doesn't make a copy of it.

Audiobooks

Press Ctrl+6 for your saved audiobooks. Press Enter on a book to see its chapters. If you've already started a chapter, BlindSpot tells you how far you got, and Enter carries on from there. Press Backspace to go back to your books.

Which audiobooks you can hear, and for how long, depends on your Spotify plan.

Recently Played

Press Ctrl+5 to see what you've listened to lately, starting with the most recent. The list loads when you move into it, and Ctrl+Shift+F refreshes it. Press Enter to play a song again. Its context menu has the usual commands for albums, the queue and your library. The first time you open Recently Played, Spotify may ask you to let BlindSpot see your listening history.

Lyrics

Press Ctrl+Y to see the lyrics for the selected song, or for the song that's playing. To search the lyrics, press Ctrl+F, or Command+F on macOS. Press Enter to find the next match and Shift+Enter to find the previous one. Back in the lyrics, press N and P to move between matches. Escape closes the search.

Reading along

Many songs have lyrics timed to the music. Tick Follow playback on braille display, and the lyrics move along on your braille display as the song plays. Each line appears about a second early to give you time to read it. If the timing doesn't suit a song, press Ctrl+Less-than to bring the lines half a second earlier, or Ctrl+Greater-than to make them half a second later. BlindSpot remembers this for next time you play that song.

On macOS, VoiceOver doesn't let BlindSpot send text to the braille display on its own. Instead, each line is sent as an announcement, which VoiceOver may speak as well. If you only want braille, mute VoiceOver's speech while you read. Reading along on macOS is still experimental.

Playing from a line

Move to a line and press Shift+Space to play the song from there. Press Space or F7 to pause and resume. On a checkbox or button, Space does its usual job. To move through the song line by line, hearing each one as you go, press Alt+Up Arrow or Alt+Down Arrow. On macOS, press Option+Command with the Up or Down Arrow.

If you're learning a song, tick Enable phrase mode. Playback then stops at the end of each line, so you can listen, sing it back and move on. Phrase mode is turned off again whenever you close the Lyrics window.

Shift+F6 and Shift+F8 jump to the previous or next verse or chorus, even when the Lyrics window is closed. BlindSpot finds the verses by looking for pauses between lines, so this only works for songs with timed lyrics. If a song doesn't have them, BlindSpot tells you. To hear the section number after each jump, turn that on in Preferences.

Karaoke and instrumental versions

BlindSpot can find lyrics for karaoke and instrumental versions too, so you can sing along. It only uses timed lyrics when they were written for a recording within ten seconds of the same length. BlindSpot remembers which lyrics it chose for that version, and any timing change you make, so you only need to get it right once.

Translating lyrics

BlindSpot can translate lyrics into your own language using DeepL. For this you need a DeepL API key, and the free one works well. To get one, open Preferences and choose Get a DeepL API key, near the end. It takes you to DeepL's sign-up page. Once you have your key, enter it in Preferences and choose a Lyrics translation language.

BlindSpot doesn't translate every song automatically, because that would quickly use up your DeepL allowance. When you'd like a translation, press Fetch translation in the Lyrics window. Once it arrives, use the list above the lyrics to switch between Original and Translation. Translations are saved, so opening the same song again costs nothing.

Carts

A cart is a short clip from a song that plays at the press of a key. It could be a jingle, a sound effect, a favourite line, or a passage you're rehearsing. You have nine of them, on Alt+1 to Alt+9. They're named after the cartridges radio stations once used for the same job.

Making a cart

  1. Play the song, and press Left Bracket where you want the clip to start.
  2. Keep listening, and press Right Bracket where you want it to end. The end has to come after the start, in the same song.
  3. Press Alt with a number from 1 to 9 to save the clip in that slot. This replaces anything that was already there.

Playing carts

To play a cart, press Alt with its number. The clip plays from its start to its end, and BlindSpot says the cart's name. If you'd rather not hear the name, you can turn it off in Preferences. If there's nothing in that slot, BlindSpot tells you.

To see all nine carts, choose File, Manage carts. Each cart shows its name and its start and end times, and you can listen to it or clear it. Repeat works with carts too. With repeat off, a cart plays once. With Repeat One, it plays over and over. With Repeat All, the carts play one after another in number order, then start again from the first.

Bookmarks

A bookmark saves your place in whatever you're listening to. To add one, press Ctrl+Shift+B. To see your bookmarks, press Ctrl+B or choose File, Bookmarks. Each bookmark tells you how far into the track you saved it. Press Enter to carry on listening from there, or Delete to remove it. The context menu has more options, such as opening the album or adding the track to the queue.

Bookmarks are kept by BlindSpot rather than Spotify, so you won't see them in other Spotify apps.

Sleep timer

Press Ctrl+Shift+J to set the sleep timer for 15, 30 or 60 minutes. When the time is up, the music stops, whether it's playing in BlindSpot or on another device.

Concerts

BlindSpot can search Ticketmaster for upcoming concerts. For this you need a Ticketmaster Discovery API key, which is free. To find out how to get one, open Preferences and choose Get a Ticketmaster API key, near the end. When you have your key, enter it in Preferences.

To search, press Ctrl+Shift+G or choose File, Search for concerts. Choose a country and a range of dates. You can also type an artist, city or state code, and choose a category and a genre. The genres on offer change to suit the category, and leaving both on All gives you the widest search. Then press Search concerts.

Concerts are listed in date order, 50 at a time. For more, choose Load more results at the end of the list. Press Enter on a concert to open its Ticketmaster page. Escape closes the window, and your search will still be there next time you open it.

Listening on other devices

BlindSpot has its own player built in, but it can also play through your phone, a smart speaker, or any other device signed in to your Spotify account. Press Ctrl+Shift+D, or choose Go, Playback device, and pick a device. You can start playing there straight away, or move what's playing but leave it paused. You can also wait, and switch over the next time you press play. To come back to BlindSpot's own player, choose BlindSpot from the same list.

When you're controlling another device, your commands travel through Spotify's servers, so it may be a little slower to respond. Some devices don't let BlindSpot change their volume, and Spotify doesn't show some devices to other apps at all.

If a device you expect isn't in the list, it's probably because Spotify doesn't think it's in use. Open Spotify on that device and play something. For a Sonos, Chromecast or similar speaker, use the Spotify app on your phone to start playing on the speaker, and leave it playing. Then choose Refresh device list at the end of the list.

Choosing a sound card on Windows

On Windows, the same list also shows the sound cards on your computer, each starting with the words Sound card. Choose one, and everything BlindSpot's own player plays comes out of it, including Spotify, podcasts and your own recordings. To go back to your normal sound card, choose Sound card: Windows default. The one you're using is marked current.

If nothing is playing when you choose, the switch happens as soon as you start playing something. BlindSpot uses the same setting as the Volume mixer in Windows' sound settings, so you'll see your choice there too. Spotify's player runs inside Microsoft Edge WebView2, which some other apps also use, so their sound may move to the same sound card.

Exporting a list

To keep a copy of a playlist or any other list, choose File, Export list. You'll be asked where to save it, and whether you'd like a plain text file or a CSV file for a spreadsheet. Each entry includes its position, title, artist, album and length, and a link to it on Spotify. Only these details are saved, never the music itself.

The file matches the list as you see it, including any filter or sort you've applied. If you're looking at a list of playlists or albums, you get the songs from the one you've selected. If the list hasn't finished loading, BlindSpot warns you that the file is incomplete. To get everything, load the rest of the list and export it again. You can export charts from Discover too, but songs that haven't been looked up on Spotify yet won't have a length or a link.

Preferences

To change how BlindSpot works, choose File, Preferences, or press Ctrl+Comma.

Playback and announcements

BlindSpot can announce each new song as it starts. This is off until you turn it on. You can also choose what BlindSpot remembers for next time. It can remember nothing, the last song you played, or the last song and how far through it you were. If it remembers, press F7 or F4 after starting BlindSpot to pick up where you left off.

BlindSpot also remembers your volume, so it starts at the level you last chose. Muting doesn't count, so you won't start in silence. When you change the volume, BlindSpot tells you the new level as a percentage. You can turn that off if you prefer.

Language

Choose the language for BlindSpot's menus and messages. If you choose System default, BlindSpot uses your computer's language, as long as a translation exists. Otherwise it uses English. Restart BlindSpot after changing the language.

BlindSpot is currently available in English and Polish. This manual is only in English for now. You don't need to be a programmer to translate BlindSpot into your own language. You'll find a guide for translators in the locale folder of the BlindSpot GitHub repository. Corrections to the Polish translation are welcome too.

Keyboard Manager

Keyboard Manager lets you change BlindSpot's shortcuts. You'll find it in Preferences. To find a command, type its name, where it's used or its current key in the search box. Commands are grouped into Main, Lists and Lyrics, depending on where they work. To give a command a new key, choose Assign. If another command in the same group already had that key, it loses it. Clear removes a command's key. Restore defaults puts the whole group back the way it was.

You can also set up global shortcuts, which work even while you're using another program. These are available for playback, skipping, volume and mute, liking the current song, hearing the song name and time, bookmarks, shuffle and repeat. None are set up at first. A global shortcut takes over its key in every program, so pick combinations your other programs don't use. If another program already uses one of your keys, BlindSpot tells you and still sets up the rest.

Logging

A log is a running record of what BlindSpot is doing, which helps when tracking down problems. Logging is off unless you turn it on. Reporting a problem, below, explains how to use it.

Reporting a problem

When something goes wrong, the error message has a Copy error details button. You can also choose Help, Copy last error details at any time. Either one copies a description of what BlindSpot was doing and what went wrong, ready to paste into an email or bug report. Passwords, keys, email addresses and your Windows user name are left out.

For trickier problems, a log helps a great deal. In Preferences, set the logging level to Debug, and make the problem happen again. Then choose Help, Capture log for developer. You'll see the whole report before anything leaves your computer, and you can copy it or save it to a file. It includes the versions of BlindSpot and your operating system, your settings and the last error. Your passwords and keys are never included.

To add the latest part of the log, tick Include recent log lines. The names of songs, artists, playlists and devices, and anything you've searched for, are hidden unless you tick the box to include them. If you do, the report shows what you've been listening to, so read it through before you send it.

Your data

On Windows, BlindSpot keeps everything in a folder called data, next to the program. That includes your settings, Spotify sign-in, bookmarks, carts, transcripts and logs. Before you give a copy of BlindSpot to someone else, choose File, Account, Sign out and erase credentials.

On macOS, BlindSpot keeps everything in your home folder, under Library/Application Support/BlindSpot. That way you stay signed in when you move or update the app.

Installing on macOS

BlindSpot isn't signed by Apple, so macOS won't open it straight away. After unzipping the download, open Terminal in the BlindSpot-macOS folder and type sh prepare-macos.sh. This tells macOS that the app is safe, and then opens it. You don't need an administrator password.

Updates

Each time BlindSpot starts, it checks for a newer version and offers to install it. On Windows, BlindSpot closes, updates itself and starts again, and your data folder is kept. On macOS, it opens the download page instead, because an app can't safely replace itself while it's running. To check for updates yourself, choose Help, Check for updates.

Supporting BlindSpot

If you find BlindSpot useful and would like to support it, choose Help, Donate to Project. This opens a PayPal donation page in your web browser.
